# Wuji environment/reference parity audit

Read-only audit of the currently installed Wuji/MJLab sources, 2026-09-16. No `AGENTS.md` was found in the project or its ancestor directories. These conclusions describe the current checkout; historical runs still need their exact Wuji/MJLab revisions matched.

**Conclusion:** the raw-action penalty and history feedback are real. I found no Wuji-specific reward/action/timeout wrapper mismatch that establishes the cause of the storms. There are normalization and startup differences, an overlooked disturbance ramp, and a plausible delayed policy-feedback mechanism worth measuring. The brainstorm's claim that negative advantages inherently widen a Gaussian is incorrect as a general explanation.

**Confirmed parity and differences**

- Both stacks instantiate the same task config and MJLab environment. rl_games forwards raw rewards, sends `policy` observations to the actor and `critic` observations to the central value network, and combines termination/truncation identically. See [mjlab_vecenv.py](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/rl_games/envs/mjlab_vecenv.py:38), [step mapping](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/rl_games/envs/mjlab_vecenv.py:114), and the reference [wrapper](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/.venv/lib/python3.11/site-packages/mjlab/rl/vecenv_wrapper.py:72). The environment itself scales reward terms by `step_dt=0.05`; rl_games' reward multiplier is 1.
- Both bootstrap a timeout with `gamma * V(current observation)` before ending the GAE chain; this is the same approximation in both implementations, not an explanation for their difference. See [rl_games](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/rl_games/common/a2c_common.py:1129) and [reference](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_rl_libs/rsl_rl/rsl_rl/algorithms/ppo.py:178). Wuji uses the default infinite-horizon flag. The rl_games wrapper would incorrectly expose timeouts for a different finite-horizon task, but that does not affect this task.
- Exploration matches: state-dependent `softplus(raw)+0.2`; rl_games' bias -1.05 initializes sigma to 0.500058 versus reference 0.5. There is no meaningful initial-scale mismatch. Reference configuration: [ppo.py](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/config/wuji_hand/rsl_rl/ppo.py:16).
- Reference training randomizes initial episode ages; rl_games starts at zero. This changes initial warmup, timeout staggering and curriculum inputs, but is weak as a standalone explanation for a late collapse. See [training entry](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/scripts/train/train_rsl_rl.py:145) and [age randomization](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_rl_libs/rsl_rl/rsl_rl/runners/on_policy_runner.py:56).
- Observation normalization differs: reference updates after every environment step, uses `(x-mean)/(std+0.01)`, and has no output clipping. rl_games freezes stats during collection, updates during every training minibatch (`set_train()` re-enables updates before each one), uses `sqrt(var+1e-5)`, and clips normalized features to ±5. See [reference updates](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_rl_libs/rsl_rl/rsl_rl/algorithms/ppo.py:159), [reference formula](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_rl_libs/rsl_rl/rsl_rl/modules/normalization.py:46), [rl_games schedule](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/rl_games/common/a2c_common.py:1619), and [rl_games formula](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/rl_games/algos_torch/running_mean_std.py:110). This is a plausible amplification channel when raw-action observations shift, not a demonstrated root cause.

**What the raw-action mechanism actually says**

The actuator clamps actions to ±1, applies scale 0.5 around the default pose, clamps joint limits, and smooths with EMA 0.5 ([actions.py](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/mdp/actions.py:33)). The penalty separately evaluates the unbounded raw quantity `sum((a-a_prev)^2 + (a-2*a_prev+a_prevprev)^2)` ([rewards.py](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/mdp/rewards.py:123)). Both actor and critic receive three history frames of raw actions ([observation config](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/reorient_terms.py:85), [history accessor](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/mdp/observations.py:197)). Physical saturation therefore does not remove feedback through observations or reward.

For one Gaussian dimension, the ascent direction for log sigma at the old policy is proportional to `A * (z^2-1)`, where `z=(a-mu)/sigma`. A negative-advantage sample near the mean increases sigma; a negative-advantage tail sample decreases it. The softplus parameterization multiplies this by the positive factor `sigmoid(raw)/sigma`, preserving the sign. Both trainers center advantages over the batch ([rl_games](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/rl_games/common/a2c_common.py:1718), [reference](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_rl_libs/rsl_rl/rsl_rl/algorithms/ppo.py:209)); negative raw returns do not imply uniformly negative policy advantages.

With independent Gaussian samples, constant mean and fixed variance, the expected first-plus-second-difference cost is `8 * D * sigma^2`. At D=20 it is 6.4 for sigma .2 and 13.456 for sigma .29. Correct optimization of this penalty pushes variance down. A one-million-sample CPU check of `r=-2*a^2`, centered/scaled advantages and sigma=.2 yielded `mean(A*(z^2-1))=-1.4167`, also shrinking sigma. This does not disprove an observed widening/penalty feedback loop; it means the loop requires evidence about advantage/action correlation, PPO updates, value error or observation feedback. It does not follow from the penalty alone.

Useful onset telemetry: mean/p99/max sigma, mean-action deltas separately from sampled-action deltas, saturation fraction, `A*(z^2-1)` by advantage sign and z-magnitude, and the realized PPO-weighted sigma gradient before/after clipping. Average entropy alone hides rare state-dependent sigma spikes. A constant saturated mean has zero difference penalty only after transients; mean magnitude is also discouraged by rl_games' bounds regularizer.

**Independent disturbance ramp remains active late**

The success curriculum being maximal does not mean the task has stopped changing. Random velocity disturbances independently ramp their upper speed from .05 toward .15 m/s, scaled by `adaptive_episode`, over iterations 250–4000 of the default 5000×40-step run. See [event parameters](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/reorient_terms.py:355), [speed computation](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/mdp/event_impl/episode.py:99), [schedule](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/mdp/event_utils.py:29), and [200k-step budget](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/wuji-mjlab/src/wuji_mjlab/tasks/reorient/mdp/event_impl/curriculum.py:20). At iteration 3719, the ceiling is about .1425 m/s if adaptive scale is 1. This could expose different robustness margins around run A's reported onset. It cannot by itself explain an onset at 4909, after the ramp has ended, and both trainers face it.

**Checkpoint-only feedback diagnostic**

Freeze the normalizer and all non-action-history observations, then estimate the Jacobian blocks from raw-action history to the actor mean on the *same captured observations* at clean, pre-storm and storm checkpoints. Use central finite differences, or manually reproduce differentiable normalization before calling the actor network: `BaseModelNetwork.norm_obs()` itself uses `no_grad`, so naïve input autograd through the wrapper will miss this derivative.

Account for the actual delays: MJLab shifts `prev <- current <- new` before producing observations, and `previous_raw_action` returns `prev`, not `current` ([action_manager.py](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/.venv/lib/python3.11/site-packages/mjlab/managers/action_manager.py:152)). Away from reset, the three history slots therefore give lags 2, 3, 4 at the next action decision (verify flattened slot ordering from the observation manager). The frozen-state linearization is `delta a_t = J2 delta a_(t-2) + J3 delta a_(t-3) + J4 delta a_(t-4)`. Assemble its 80×80 companion matrix for D=20. Spectral radius >1 indicates local deterministic feedback amplification. `||J2||+||J3||+||J4||<1` in a common induced norm is a sufficient contraction condition. Also measure short-horizon matrix-power gain: non-normal systems can transiently amplify despite spectral radius <1.

Run a 100–200-step synthetic history recurrence with frozen physical features, using deterministic means and then common random noise at fixed sigma .2; compare growth across checkpoints. This isolates mean feedback from optimizer and simulator behavior. It is a local diagnostic, not proof of full closed-loop stability; physical-state feedback, moving normalization, nonlinear clipping and changing Jacobians remain outside this frozen test. Finite differences near ±5 normalized clipping boundaries should use multiple step sizes.

**Comparable experiment matrix**

Keep task revision, seed set, actor/critic size, 8192×40 rollout, 5000 iterations and raw-action task unchanged for the primary comparison. Resume-only tests require environment/curriculum state restoration; ordinary model checkpoints may restart the disturbance schedule, which would confound an onset comparison.

**Metric correction:** the installed reward manager divides `Episode_Reward/*` by the configured maximum episode duration (50 s), not each actual episode duration ([reward_manager.py](/home/viktor/Projects/Research/rl_games/.venv/lib/python3.11/site-packages/mjlab/managers/reward_manager.py:100)). Thus the brainstorm's conversion from logged rate to per-step cost is accurate only near full-length episodes. During frequent early drops, report episode lengths or direct per-step term means alongside this metric.
